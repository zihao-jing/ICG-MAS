## What the labeller column means

The v2 prompt defines DEFECT as **answer wrong and a critical fact absent or distorted at a checkpoint**. A wrong answer whose evidence is faithful at every checkpoint (a computation or aggregation error) falls outside both labels, and the model resolves it to CORRECT. It also resolves some exact-token mismatches to CORRECT when the gold entity is present. Both behaviours were present in the validation set (4 of the 7 disagreements); this population has many more such traces. The 89 lenient cases split as follows:

| benchmark | mechanism | traces |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| MQ | contains the gold plus extra words | 46 |
| MQ | other | 7 |
| MQ | wrong answer, but no checkpoint defect | 29 |
| SB | wrong answer, but no checkpoint defect | 7 |

Of the 89, **46** are answers that contain the gold entity plus extra words, which the prompt's own normalization rule calls DEFECT, and **36** are wrong answers with faithful evidence (computation or aggregation errors, plus a few near-miss values). The remaining 7 include explicit self-contradictions that state the strict rule gives DEFECT and then return CORRECT. The CORRECT rate therefore overstates answer accuracy by **+20.3 points on MQ** (44.1% against 23.8% exact) and **+3.4 points on SB** (19.7% against 16.3%). The MQ number is close to the paper's mean-F1 cell (46.7), but it is not the same quantity: mean F1 gives partial credit, while the labeller is a binary evidence-flow label.

## Reading

This pilot does **not** support re-scoring the main-table task scores with the v2 labeller as it stands. 89 of 607 wrong answers are called CORRECT, and the two mechanisms above are systematic, not noise. The original lexical cells and the paper numbers are unchanged. A defensible re-score needs either (a) a separate answer-equivalence prompt validated on the same 150 traces before any full run, or (b) reporting the v2 label only as an evidence-flow quantity beside the lexical cells, not as accuracy. No full run was started.
